A franchised car dealership does not only sell vehicles. It also works under a written agreement with an automaker, and that agreement can include standards the dealership has to meet. Anyone trying to understand how dealerships operate, or what makes one more or less secure as a business, should know how these standards work.

The sections below cover how performance standards work, how they can affect a franchise, and what the law says about non-renewal.

## What a franchise agreement is

Most dealerships sell new cars under a franchise agreement with an automaker or its authorized distributor. Economists describe the dealer as the franchisee and the automaker as the franchisor, according to the Wikipedia article on car dealerships. The same reference notes that either side can act opportunistically after the other has made sunk investments, which is one reason the terms of the relationship are written down. One of the things a manufacturer can require is that the dealership performs at a certain level.

## What performance standards cover

AutoNation, a large publicly traded dealership group, describes this in its annual report to the Securities and Exchange Commission for fiscal year 2025. It states that manufacturers can set performance standards with respect to sales volume, sales effectiveness, and customer satisfaction.

The report names these three areas but does not publish the exact targets or define each measure, and the details are set in the agreement with each manufacturer. What it does show is that the standards are not limited to sales. Customer satisfaction is named alongside sales volume and sales effectiveness.

## What happens when a dealership falls short

AutoNation states that from time to time certain manufacturers assert sales and customer satisfaction performance deficiencies. The consequences it lists fall into two groups.

The first group concerns growth. AutoNation says its framework agreements contain requirements about its operating performance, and that failing them can limit further acquisitions or force divestitures. It also says it is precluded under agreements with certain manufacturers from acquiring additional franchises when performance criteria are not met. In plain terms, a dealership group that misses its standards may not be allowed to add more stores of that brand.

The second group concerns the franchise itself. AutoNation says its agreements provide for termination by the manufacturer, or non-renewal, for a variety of causes. It lists performance deficiencies in sales volume, sales effectiveness, and customer satisfaction or loyalty among those causes. It also lists uncured performance deficiencies and unapproved changes of ownership or management as grounds for a manufacturer to terminate or compel the sale of a franchise.

:::note A sourced fact on ownership changes
The AutoNation report names an unapproved change of ownership or management as a ground for termination.
:::

## The legal protection dealers have

A manufacturer does not have unlimited freedom to end a franchise. The AutoNation report states that it is unlawful for a manufacturer to terminate or not renew a franchise unless good cause exists. It also says dealership franchise agreements are rarely involuntarily terminated or not renewed by the manufacturer.

A general reference on dealerships, the Wikipedia article on car dealerships, says that all US states severely limit the ability of a manufacturer to terminate a franchise, and that most states require manufacturers to buy back inventory and special equipment when a franchise is terminated. These laws vary by state, so the details for any specific dealership depend on where it is located and on its own agreement.

The practical result is that performance standards matter, but a missed target does not automatically end a franchise. The AutoNation wording refers to uncured deficiencies as a ground for termination, and the law requires good cause.

## How long agreements last

AutoNation says its franchise agreements have various durations, ranging from one year to no expiration date. In certain cases manufacturers have undertaken to renew franchises upon expiration. The company also says it expects routine renewals without substantial cost or modification, but it adds that it cannot guarantee every agreement will be renewed.

That last point is worth noticing. Even a large, well-established dealership group states openly that renewal is not guaranteed.

## What this means when you look at a dealership

If you are trying to understand a dealership business, performance standards are one reason to look beyond the number of cars sold. Sales volume, sales effectiveness and customer satisfaction can each affect whether a dealership keeps its franchise or is allowed to expand. Each of the three areas is named as a possible cause for action against a dealership.

## Summary

Franchised dealerships operate under agreements that can set standards for sales volume, sales effectiveness and customer satisfaction. Falling short can limit the ability of a dealership group to add franchises and, if the problem is not cured, can be a cause for termination or non-renewal. State law gives dealers significant protection, and the manufacturer needs good cause. Renewal is still not guaranteed, even for large groups.
