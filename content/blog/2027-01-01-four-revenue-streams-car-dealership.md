Most people think of a car dealership as a place that sells cars. The reports that dealers publish describe something broader. A dealership runs four lines of business, and each one works differently. This article names the four lines and shows how much of 2025 dealership sales each one brought in. The figures come from the National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA) and from the annual report of AutoNation, a public dealer group.

## The four lines of a dealership

AutoNation's annual report for fiscal year 2025 describes its business in four parts: new vehicles, used vehicles, parts and service, and finance and insurance. A single store can be described with the same four lines.

New vehicles are cars and trucks sold under the automaker's brand that the dealer holds a franchise for. Used vehicles are pre-owned cars sold by the same dealer. Parts and service covers repair and maintenance work plus parts sales. Finance and insurance covers the products a customer can add when buying a vehicle, such as financing and protection plans.

## New vehicle sales

One way AutoNation states that it acquires new vehicles for retail sale is directly from the applicable automaker. That is the starting point for the new vehicle line: the automaker supplies vehicles and the dealer sells them to customers.

For fiscal year 2025, AutoNation reported new vehicle revenues of $13,501.3 million and 259,264 retail new vehicle units sold. A unit is one vehicle. Counting units tells a reader how many vehicles were sold, which a dollar figure alone does not show.

## Used vehicle sales

Used vehicles reach a dealer in a different way. AutoNation states that it generally acquires used vehicles from customers, primarily through trade-ins. A customer who buys a new vehicle and trades in the old one adds a used vehicle to the dealer's supply. That link connects the new vehicle line to the used vehicle line.

## Parts and service

AutoNation describes this line as including automotive repair and maintenance services as well as wholesale parts and collision businesses. A customer who brings a vehicle in for an oil change, a repair or body work is using this line. A repair shop or body shop that buys parts from the dealer is using it too.

This line is defined by the work done on vehicles and the parts sold, not by the sale of a vehicle itself. That is why annual reports describe it apart from the two vehicle lines.

## Finance and insurance

AutoNation describes its finance and insurance line as automotive finance and insurance products, which include vehicle service and other protection products. These are offered when a customer buys a vehicle.

These products are sold alongside a vehicle, so they appear in the same transaction as a new or used sale. AutoNation reports them as their own line, which lets a reader see them apart from the vehicle itself.

:::note What the NADA data covers
The NADA report gives 2025 total dealership sales of $1,301,478 million, about $1.3 trillion. It divides those sales dollars among new vehicles, used vehicles, and service and parts. It does not give a separate sales figure for finance and insurance.
:::

## How much each line brought in

The NADA full-year report for 2025 gives these shares of total dealership sales dollars:

New vehicles were 54.9 percent. Used vehicles were 31.8 percent. Service and parts were 13.3 percent.

New vehicles brought in more than half of dealership sales dollars. Used vehicles brought in a little under a third. Service and parts brought in 13.3 percent, which is a little over one eighth. Put another way, vehicle sales, new and used together, made up 86.7 percent of the sales dollars in the NADA report. These shares measure sales dollars, which is the amount customers paid. They do not measure profit.

## Why sales and profit are different measures

Gross profit is what a sale brings in after the cost of what was sold is subtracted. A line with a large share of sales can have a different share of gross profit, because the cost of goods differs from line to line. The NADA figures above are sales shares only, so they should not be read as profit shares.

## What to take from the four lines

Knowing the four lines gives a reader a way to read any dealership report. AutoNation breaks its business out along these lines, so a useful first question about any number in a dealer report is which line it belongs to.

Three points to keep in mind. First, the four lines are different kinds of work: selling vehicles, selling used vehicles, repairing and supplying parts, and selling finance and insurance products. Second, the share of sales dollars is not the share of profit. Third, the NADA shares describe dealerships across the country in 2025, so the mix at any one store may be different.
