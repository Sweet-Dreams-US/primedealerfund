Most people think of a car dealership as a place that sells cars. The showroom is the part customers see. Behind it, a dealership runs several other lines of business, and two of them are easy to overlook: wholesale parts and collision repair, also called the body shop.

This article explains what those two lines are and what the public numbers say about the parts and service side of a dealership. It is general education about auto retail. It is not investment advice, and it does not describe the results of any particular fund.

## The parts and service side of a dealership

Dealerships earn money in more than one way. They sell new vehicles, sell used vehicles, arrange financing and insurance products, and keep customers' cars running through service and parts. The service and parts side is often called after-sales or fixed operations.

AutoNation, a large public dealership group, describes its parts and service line in its annual report as including "automotive repair and maintenance services as well as wholesale parts and collision businesses." That one sentence names the pieces. Repair and maintenance happen in the service bays. Wholesale parts and collision are the two additions this article covers.

The same filing notes that the company performs both customer-pay work and manufacturer warranty work in its stores. Customer-pay work is paid for by the vehicle owner. Warranty work is paid for by the manufacturer under the terms of the vehicle warranty.

## What wholesale parts means

A dealership keeps a parts department stocked for its own repair work. When a technician needs a part, it comes from that inventory. That is the retail side of parts, used for the dealership's own service customers.

Wholesale parts is different. It means selling parts to other businesses instead of to a retail customer at the service counter. Independent repair shops and body shops are common buyers. For the dealership, it is a way to put its parts inventory and purchasing relationships to more use.

The NADA Data report shows how large the parts category is for new-vehicle dealerships as a group. For 2025, parts sales totaled $93.42 billion. Service labor sales totaled $75.81 billion. NADA reports that parts sales came to $1.43 for every dollar of service labor sales. In other words, parts are the larger part of service and parts sales across the industry.

## What collision work means

A collision center, or body shop, repairs vehicles after an accident. The work includes repairing body panels, refinishing paint and restoring the vehicle to its pre-accident condition. Many of these jobs are paid through an insurance claim, though some are paid directly by the owner.

The NADA report tracks body shop repair orders in three groups: customer, claim and internal. A customer order is one the owner pays for. A claim order is paid through an insurance claim. An internal order is work the dealership does on its own vehicles.

Not every dealership has a body shop. Running one takes space, equipment and trained staff, so some dealers choose to leave it out. As of December 31, 2025, AutoNation reported that it owned and operated 52 AutoNation-branded collision centers.

:::note Worth knowing
AutoNation's 2025 annual report states that the company owned and operated 52 AutoNation-branded collision centers as of December 31, 2025. The filing describes collision as part of the same after-sales line as wholesale parts and repair.
:::

## How big is the whole service and parts line

NADA's 2025 full-year report puts total service and parts sales for new-vehicle dealerships at about $164.6 billion. That is an average of $9,687,942 per dealership. Service and parts were 13.3 percent of total dealership sales dollars in 2025, up from 13.2 percent in 2024.

That share is of sales dollars, not of gross profit. The NADA page does not give the share of gross profit, so this article does not state one. Any reader comparing lines of business should keep that difference in mind, since a line can be a small share of sales and a larger share of profit, or the other way around.

The report also counts repair orders. Dealerships wrote 276,128,228 repair orders in total in 2025, which is about 16,252 per dealership. Service and parts sales averaged $494 per customer repair order and $551 per warranty repair order. The average customer mechanical labor rate was $186.

:::note Worth knowing
NADA reports that 50.4 percent of new light-vehicle dealerships offered express service operations in 2025. Express service is a faster lane for short jobs such as routine maintenance.
:::

## Why these lines matter to a dealership

Sales of vehicles move up and down with the market. Parts and service tie to the vehicles already on the road. A car that was sold years ago still needs maintenance and, eventually, repairs. That gives the parts and service side a different relationship to the sales cycle than vehicle sales have.

Parts and collision add to that line in their own ways. Wholesale parts uses the parts inventory the dealership already keeps. Collision work brings in vehicles that need repair after accidents, rather than routine maintenance. Each depends on different customers, so they do not rise and fall for the same reasons.

This is a description of how the lines work, not a forecast. How much any one dealership earns from parts or collision depends on its size, its location, its staff and its manufacturer. The industry figures above are averages across many dealerships.

## How to read a dealership group's numbers

If you read a dealership group's annual report, look for how it describes its lines of business. AutoNation's report groups repair, wholesale parts and collision under one heading. Some companies may break them out differently, so check the definitions before comparing two companies.

It also helps to check what a figure measures. Sales dollars, gross profit and repair order counts are different things. A report that gives one and not the others only tells part of the story. Use the full document and read the notes that explain how the numbers were built.

## Key points

Parts and collision are two lines that sit inside the after-sales side of a dealership, next to repair and maintenance. Wholesale parts sells parts to other businesses. Collision work repairs vehicles after accidents. Industry data from NADA shows that service and parts were 13.3 percent of total dealership sales dollars in 2025, and that parts sales were larger than service labor sales. Those are industry averages and public company disclosures, and they say nothing about any single dealership.
