A car dealership looks like one business from the street, but it earns money in several different ways. When a dealer group is publicly traded, it has to explain those ways in a yearly filing. That filing is one of the best free classrooms for anyone who wants to understand how dealerships work.

This article walks through how one public company, AutoNation, describes each of its revenue lines in its annual report for fiscal year 2025. The goal is to learn how to read a report like this, not to judge any company or to suggest any investment.

## Why a public filing is a good place to start

Public companies in the United States file an annual report called a Form 10-K with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The 10-K has a business description near the front, which is labeled Item 1. It explains in the company's own words what the company sells and how.

For a dealer group, that description is organized around the revenue lines. If you want to learn how a dealership is built, reading how a large group splits its business is a clear way to see the pieces.

## Line one: new vehicles

New vehicle sales are the line most people think of first. In its fiscal 2025 report, AutoNation shows new vehicle revenue of $13,501.3 million, from 259,264 retail new vehicle units sold.

The filing also explains where the inventory comes from. The stores acquire new vehicles directly from manufacturers or distributors, or through dealer trades with other stores of the same brand.

:::note Worth knowing
AutoNation reported new vehicle revenue of $13,501.3 million for 2025, from 259,264 retail new vehicle units. Source: AutoNation Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025.
:::

Notice what that sentence tells you. It gives a dollar total and a unit count. Dividing one by the other gives a rough sense of the revenue per vehicle sold. A reader should treat that as a starting point only, since the filing is the place to check how the company defines each figure.

## Line two: used vehicles

The used vehicle line works differently, because the inventory comes from a different place. The filing says the stores mainly acquire used vehicles through trade-ins and through a program called "We'll Buy Your Car." The vehicles are reconditioned in the stores' parts and service departments.

Not every used vehicle is sold to a retail customer. According to the filing, units that are not sold at retail are sold wholesale at auction.

This shows how the lines connect. According to the filing, used inventory comes mainly from trade-ins, and the reconditioning is done in the parts and service departments. One line feeds the other.

## Line three: parts and service

The third line is parts and service, which AutoNation also calls "After-Sales." The filing describes it as repair and maintenance, wholesale parts and collision work.

This line is different in kind from the first two. New and used sales happen when a customer buys a vehicle. Repair and maintenance work can happen again and again over the years that someone owns a vehicle. When you read a dealer group's report, this is the line to study to understand how a dealership earns money from vehicles it sold in the past.

## Line four: finance and insurance

The fourth line is finance and insurance, which AutoNation also calls "Customer Financial Services." According to the filing, it includes vehicle protection products and arranging third-party vehicle financing for a commission.

In the arrangement the filing describes, the financing comes from a third party. The dealer arranges it and earns a commission.

:::note Worth knowing
AutoNation describes finance and insurance as including vehicle protection products and the arranging of third-party vehicle financing for a commission. Source: AutoNation Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025.
:::

## How to read the numbers

The filing states that charts showing each line's share of 2025 total revenue and gross profit appear in Item 1. Revenue and gross profit are not the same thing, and a line that is large by revenue can be small by gross profit, or the other way around. When you read any dealer group's report, look at both measures for each line, not only the total.

The detailed revenue and profit tables sit in a later part of the filing, in the sections that discuss financial results and the financial statements. Item 1 gives the plain description. The later sections give the amounts for each line.

A practical way to study a report like this is to write down the four lines on a sheet of paper. Under each one, note how the company gets its inventory or its work, what the company says about the line, and what dollar figures it reports. Doing this for two or three companies makes the pattern easy to see.

## Keep the limits in mind

A public company's filing describes that company. A single dealer group is not every dealer group, and the numbers in one year are not a forecast for the next. The filing is a source of facts about how the business is reported, and it is useful for learning vocabulary and structure.
